This family is where rhythm and voice carry the song, and where tempo is a poor guide because
the interesting movement is in the subdivision rather than the pulse.

Pop is a production standard more than a sound: heavy compression, a chorus by 50 seconds,
3-4 minutes long, 100-130 BPM. Hip hop is lyric-forward, usually 80-100 BPM, and its
energy figure is routinely underestimated by audio features because density lives in the
vocal rather than the instrumental. R&B sits slower, 65-85 BPM, with the highest
sensuality and the most harmonic detail of the group. Afrobeats at 100-110 BPM and
reggaeton at 90-100 both carry extremely high danceability at only moderate tempo, because
the groove is in the off-beats - which is exactly why tempo-matching alone fails for them.

Bedroom pop and indie pop are the lo-fi corner of this family: deliberately imperfect,
more acoustic texture, lower popularity, and usually more melancholy than their parent genre.

For the recommender this family exposes the broad-versus-narrow label problem directly:
pop, indie pop and bedroom pop share a word and score a partial genre match, but a
listener who asks for bedroom pop and is handed stadium pop has not been served.
