The counter-intuitive and well-replicated finding is that people in distress do not want
cheering up by their music. Given a free choice, sad listeners select sad music, and report
feeling better afterwards. The mechanism appears to be validation and perceived company
rather than mood repair, which means a system that helpfully substitutes upbeat songs for
a heartbreak request is making the output worse while appearing to be kind.

So match the feeling. Low energy, slow tempo, acoustic and sparse, with lyrics that name the
feeling - this is one of the few contexts where vocals are a feature rather than a cost,
because being told your experience out loud is the point.

The one real guardrail is intensity rather than sadness: cathartic and defiant material
(loud, angry, fast) serves a different need - anger rather than grief - and should not be
mixed into a grief list without being asked for. Keep the arc flat. A list that climbs toward
euphoria reads as being hurried out of a feeling.
