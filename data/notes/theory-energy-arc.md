A playlist is not a set, it is a sequence, and the ordering carries information the song
scores do not. Three arcs cover almost every request.

A build starts at roughly 60-70% of the target energy and rises to the peak at the end.
It suits workouts, mornings, parties and any context where the listener's arousal starts
below where they want it. The rule of thumb is that each track should be at or above the
previous one, allowing one dip, because a strictly monotonic climb feels mechanical.

A descend inverts this, and suits sleep, winding down and the end of a long day. Here
the final track should be the quietest and slowest in the list, and the descent should be
monotonic - a single loud track late in a wind-down list undoes the whole sequence.

A flat arc holds energy within a narrow window throughout, and is correct for focus,
driving and most emotional contexts. Flat does not mean identical: a window of about plus
or minus 0.1 energy reads as consistent, while plus or minus 0.3 reads as erratic.

Checking an arc is cheap and worth doing automatically: compute the energy sequence, fit the
trend, and compare it to the arc the request implies. An ordering failure is invisible in
per-song scores and obvious to a listener in fifteen seconds.
