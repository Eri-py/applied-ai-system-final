A wind-down playlist is the one case where the music should get measurably worse at holding
attention as it goes on. The target is entrainment: tempo near or below the resting heart
rate, around 60-70 BPM, so breathing and pulse drift down to meet it. Anything above about
80 BPM works against this.

Energy belongs very low, around 0.2, and acousticness very high. Reverberant, slow-attack
sounds - piano, strings, soft pads, field recordings - read as distance and safety, while
close-miked compressed drums read as presence and alertness. Percussion with a hard transient
is the main thing to avoid: a kick drum is a startle stimulus at low volume.

Lyrics are a weaker problem here than in focus listening, but narrative lyrics still invite
the listener to follow a story, which is arousal. Instrumental or wordless vocals are safer.
Unlike every other context, a descending energy arc matters more than the average energy:
the last track should be the quietest thing in the list.
