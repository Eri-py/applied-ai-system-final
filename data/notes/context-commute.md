Commuting is partially-attended listening: the listener has a task, but the task uses spatial
and motor attention rather than verbal attention, so lyrics are fine here in a way they are
not during deep work. This is why podcasts and lyric-heavy music both work in cars and
neither works while writing.

The mid-energy band, roughly 0.6-0.75, is the sweet spot. Too low and a driver's alertness
genuinely drops on a long motorway stretch; too high and the music encourages speed, an
effect measurable enough that it shows up in road-safety studies.

Road noise is the hidden constraint. A car at speed produces 65-75 dB of broadband low
frequency noise, which masks quiet acoustic detail completely - a delicate solo piano piece
is simply inaudible at 70 mph. Denser, compressed, mid-forward production survives the
environment. Night driving is its own sub-case, where synthwave and cinematic material
earn their reputation: the visual field narrows, and slower, wider, more atmospheric music
matches it.
