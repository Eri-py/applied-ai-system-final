"Rainy day" is a texture request disguised as a weather request. What listeners reliably mean
is high acousticness, audible room sound, and a slow tempo - music that sounds like it was
recorded in a physical space rather than assembled in a grid. Rain itself is broadband noise
at low level, so music that competes with it in the high frequencies feels harsh, and music
with warmth in the low mids feels like it belongs.

The emotional target is not sadness but containment: nostalgic, tender and cosy rather than
grieving. There is a real distinction between a rainy-day request and a heartbreak request,
and conflating them is a common failure. A rainy Sunday wants warmth and company; heartbreak
wants recognition. Energy around 0.3-0.4, tempo 70-90, acoustic texture strongly preferred.

Lofi, folk, jazz and solo piano all serve this well. Instrumental is welcome but not
required, because the listener is usually not concentrating on anything.
