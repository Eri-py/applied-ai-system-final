Electronic music separates most cleanly by tempo, and the tempo tells you the function.

House sits at 120-128 BPM with a four-on-the-floor kick - a beat on every quarter note.
It is built for sustained dancing and its structure is deliberately repetitive so a crowd can
stay in it. Drum and bass runs at 170-180 BPM over a half-time bassline, so it feels both
frantic and heavy; it reads as very high energy and suits sprinting far better than dancing.
Synthwave sits lower, 100-115 BPM, and is defined by its palette rather than its tempo:
analogue-style pads, gated reverb, arpeggios, a deliberately 1980s cinematic nostalgia. It is
the genre most associated with night driving. Ambient often has no stable pulse at all,
which is the point - without a beat there is nothing to entrain to, so it neither energises
nor paces, and it suits sleep and background listening.

All four are almost definitionally low in acousticness, so a listener who asks for electronic
material and a listener who asks for acoustic material are asking for opposite textures, and
a profile that scores both highly is incoherent.
