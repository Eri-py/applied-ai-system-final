Training music is chosen for its physical effect rather than its meaning. Two attributes
carry nearly all of the weight: energy and tempo. Research on exercise playlists keeps
finding the same band - roughly 125 to 160 BPM for sustained cardio, drifting higher for
sprint intervals and lower for strength work, where lifters prefer heavy, slow, aggressive
material over fast material.

Acousticness is actively unwanted here. An acoustic guitar has a soft transient and a
decaying envelope; a compressed drum machine has a hard transient and a flat envelope, and
the hard transient is what a runner locks their cadence to. So a workout profile should
score produced texture, not acoustic texture.

Energy should be read as a floor rather than a target in this context. A 0.98-energy metal
track is not "too energetic" for a workout the way it would be too energetic for a commute.
Genre is close to irrelevant: metal, drum and bass, house and hard pop all serve the same
function, which is why a workout request is one of the few cases where genre should be
allowed to lose to energy.
